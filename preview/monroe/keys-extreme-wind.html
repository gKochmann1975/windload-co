Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers to Florida Keys wind load engineering questions

What is the design wind speed for the Florida Keys?
The Florida Keys fall within ASCE 7-22 Risk Category II design wind speeds ranging from 180 to 195 MPH, depending on specific location. Key West and surrounding areas require 180 MPH ultimate design wind speed, while portions of the Upper Keys may require up to 195 MPH for critical facilities. These are 3-second gust speeds at 33 feet above ground in Exposure C conditions.
Why are Florida Keys wind requirements higher than mainland Florida?
The Florida Keys experience higher wind requirements due to three factors: 1) Geographic exposure with minimal terrain roughness over open water, 2) Exposure Category D conditions for most structures near the coastline, and 3) Historical hurricane intensity data showing the Keys sustain more direct hurricane impacts. The narrow island chain provides virtually no natural wind protection.
What exposure category applies to Florida Keys structures?
Most Florida Keys structures fall under Exposure Category D per ASCE 7-22, which applies to flat, unobstructed areas exposed to wind flowing over open water for a distance of at least 1 mile. This results in significantly higher wind pressure coefficients than inland locations. Unlike mainland structures that may qualify for Exposure B or C, Keys buildings rarely have sufficient surrounding development to reduce the exposure category.
How do wind loads in the Keys compare to Miami-Dade?
While Miami-Dade requires 175-180 MPH design wind speeds, the Florida Keys face similar or higher requirements but with crucial differences: Exposure Category D vs. C for many Keys locations increases effective pressure by 15-25%, and the narrow island geography provides zero wind shadow protection. A 180 MPH design speed in the Keys produces higher actual pressures than the same speed in Miami-Dade due to the exposure coefficient differences.
What special considerations exist for Keys stilt homes?
Florida Keys elevated structures face compound loading: extreme wind speeds combined with flood zone requirements mandate minimum elevation heights often exceeding 12-15 feet. Wind loads on exposed pilings and lateral bracing must account for wave action, debris impact, and the full exposure of the elevated structure to unobstructed wind flow. The open area beneath elevated structures creates negative pressure differentials that must be addressed in the foundation design.
