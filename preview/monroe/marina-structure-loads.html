Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers on marina wind load engineering

What wind loads apply to boat lift structures?
Boat lifts in the Florida Keys require design for 180+ MPH winds with Exposure Category D. The lift structure, cables, and anchoring must resist both wind on the structure and wind on the stored vessel. ASCE 7-22 treats boat lifts as 'other structures' requiring component and cladding analysis for the canopy plus MWFRS loads for the frame. When a vessel is stored on the lift, add projected area wind load for the boat.
How are floating dock wind loads calculated?
Floating docks experience wind loads transmitted through mooring connections. Calculate wind load on exposed freeboard plus any structures (handrails, ramps, gangways). The wave action component adds dynamic loading. Pile guides must resist accumulated loads from multiple dock sections while allowing vertical movement. Mooring hardware and connections are often the critical elements.
What special requirements apply to marina fuel facilities?
Marina fuel facilities require Importance Factor of 1.15 (Risk Category III) due to hazardous contents. All structural connections must be designed for 180 MPH conditions without failure. Canopy structures over fuel dispensers require additional analysis for concentrated uplift at edges and internal pressure from partially enclosed conditions. Fire suppression and containment systems must remain functional after hurricane events.
How does wave action combine with wind loads on marina structures?
Wave-induced oscillation creates fatigue loading on marina piles and decking. ASCE 7-22 requires combining wave loads per Chapter 5 with wind loads. For vertical-pile supported structures, lateral wave forces add directly to wind forces. Floating structures experience different load paths where wave motion affects mooring line tensions rather than structure directly. The combination often controls pile embedment and connection design.
What exposure category applies to marina structures?
Marina structures in the Florida Keys uniformly fall under Exposure Category D per ASCE 7-22. This applies to flat, unobstructed areas exposed to wind flowing over open water for at least 1 mile. The Kz coefficient for Exposure D at typical marina elevations (15-25 feet) ranges from 1.03 to 1.16, significantly higher than inland conditions. No reductions are available for surrounding development.
