Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers on corrosion and wind connections

How does salt air corrosion affect hurricane clips and straps?
Standard galvanized hurricane clips can lose 50% of their rated capacity within 10-15 years in Florida Keys conditions. Salt air accelerates zinc coating loss, leading to base metal corrosion at fastener points. The HVHZ requires stainless steel or heavily galvanized connectors with G185 minimum coating for coastal applications. Once the protective coating fails, corrosion accelerates exponentially.
What fastener materials are required for Florida Keys coastal construction?
Florida Building Code Section 2304.10.5.1 requires stainless steel (Type 304 or 316) or hot-dip galvanized (G185/G235) fasteners within 3,000 feet of saltwater. Type 316 stainless is preferred for structures within 300 feet of the ocean. Electroplated zinc fasteners are prohibited in coastal applications due to rapid coating failure.
How often should wind connections be inspected for corrosion?
Florida Keys wind connections should be inspected annually for visible corrosion, with detailed inspection every 5 years by a licensed engineer. After any hurricane event, immediate inspection is required. Signs of concern include rust staining, white powdery deposits on aluminum, and loose fasteners from wood deterioration around corroded connectors.
What is galvanic corrosion and why does it matter for wind connections?
Galvanic corrosion occurs when dissimilar metals contact each other in the presence of saltwater electrolyte. In wind connections, this commonly happens when stainless steel straps contact galvanized framing anchors, or aluminum flashing contacts steel connectors. The galvanic series determines which metal will corrode sacrificially - the more "active" metal corrodes to protect the more "noble" metal.
How does corrosion reduce wind load capacity of connections?
Corrosion reduces capacity through three mechanisms: 1) Cross-sectional area loss of steel plates and straps reduces tensile/shear capacity proportionally, 2) Fastener corrosion reduces embedment pull-out values as the effective diameter decreases, 3) Wood deterioration around corroded fasteners creates enlarged holes that reduce withdrawal resistance. A 20% reduction in connector thickness results in approximately 20% capacity loss.
