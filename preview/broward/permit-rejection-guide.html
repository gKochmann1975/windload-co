Top 12 Rejection Reasons

Missing or Incorrect Wind Speed

#1
Problem

Using outdated wind speed (150 MPH) or wrong Risk Category. Broward County HVHZ requires 170-180 MPH per ASCE 7-22.

Solution

Verify wind speed using ASCE 7 Hazard Tool with exact project coordinates. Document Risk Category II (or applicable) in calculations.

Wrong Exposure Category

#2
Problem

Defaulting to Exposure C when site is within 1 mile of coast (requires Exposure D) or using B in open terrain.

Solution

Include satellite image showing upwind fetch. Document surface roughness in 8 cardinal directions per ASCE 7-22 Section 26.7.

Product Approval Mismatch

#3
Problem

Product DP rating lower than calculated design pressure, or NOA/FBC approval doesn't match specified installation method.

Solution

Include NOA/FBC approval letter with submittal. Verify product DP rating exceeds max calculated pressure in ALL zones.

Missing GCp or GCpi Values

#4
Problem

Calculations don't show external pressure coefficients (GCp) or internal pressure coefficients (GCpi) used.

Solution

Show GCp from ASCE 7-22 Figure 30.3-1 and GCpi from Table 26.13-1. Enclosed building GCpi = +/- 0.18.

Incorrect Effective Wind Area

#5
Problem

Using product area instead of effective wind area, or not accounting for span2/3 rule for cladding.

Solution

Calculate effective wind area as greater of: tributary area, or (span)^2/3 per ASCE 7-22 Section 26.2 definition.

Missing Zone Delineation

#6
Problem

No elevation drawing showing Zones 4, 5, 4' and 5' (corners and edges) with 'a' dimension labeled.

Solution

Include elevations showing pressure zones. Calculate 'a' = 10% of least horizontal or 0.4h, min 3 ft, max 0.04 x least dim.

No PE Seal or Signature

#7
Problem

Wind load calculations submitted without Florida-licensed PE seal, signature, and date for commercial projects.

Solution

All commercial wind load calcs require FL PE seal. For residential, prescriptive calcs may be accepted - verify with building dept.

Height Measurement Error

#8
Problem

Using roof peak height when mean roof height required, or measuring from wrong datum (grade vs base flood elevation).

Solution

Use mean roof height h = (eave + ridge)/2 for gabled roofs. Measure from ground or BFE in flood zones per ASCE 7-22 26.3.

Incomplete Fastener Schedule

#9
Problem

No fastener type, spacing, or embedment depth specified. Or fastener pattern doesn't match product approval.

Solution

Include complete fastener schedule matching NOA/FBC approval. Specify type, size, spacing, edge distance, and min embedment.

Debris Impact Not Addressed

#10
Problem

Glazing calculations don't include wind-borne debris protection requirements for HVHZ (all of Broward County).

Solution

Specify impact-rated glazing or approved shutters per FBC 1626.1. Include missile level (D for Broward) in product selection.

Overhang Pressures Missing

#11
Problem

Roof overhang, soffit, or fascia pressures not calculated. These areas experience different (often higher) pressures.

Solution

Calculate overhang pressures using ASCE 7-22 Figure 30.3-2A. Add both top and bottom surface pressures for net load.

Code Edition Not Specified

#12
Problem

Calculations don't specify which code edition (FBC 8th Edition 2023, ASCE 7-22) was used, or use outdated editions.

Solution

State applicable codes on cover sheet: "Per FBC 8th Edition (2023), ASCE 7-22". Verify current code adoption with Broward County.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to resubmit after a rejection?
Once you receive rejection comments from Broward County, you can typically resubmit corrections within 1-3 business days if you have accurate calculations ready. The review queue then adds another 5-10 business days. Using compliant calculations from the start eliminates this delay entirely.
Can I appeal a permit rejection?
Yes, you can request a meeting with the plans examiner to discuss the rejection. However, most rejections are due to legitimate code compliance issues, not interpretation differences. It's almost always faster and cheaper to correct the calculations than to appeal. Save appeals for cases where you genuinely believe the reviewer misinterpreted the code.
Do all Broward County municipalities use the same standards?
All municipalities within Broward County follow the Florida Building Code, but some have additional local amendments. For example, Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, and Pompano Beach may have specific submittal requirements beyond the base FBC. Always verify local requirements with the specific municipality's building department.
What's the difference between a correction and a rejection?
A correction (or "revision required") means the permit application is held while you address specific issues - your place in the review queue is typically preserved. A full rejection means the application is returned and you must resubmit from scratch, going back to the end of the review queue. Multiple corrections on the same issue may result in rejection.
Are pre-engineered calculations accepted in Broward County?
Yes, many product manufacturers provide pre-engineered calculations or load tables that are accepted for residential projects when the product has current NOA or FBC approval. For commercial projects or non-standard applications, site-specific calculations by a Florida-licensed PE are typically required.
