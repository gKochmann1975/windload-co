Frequently Asked Questions

Why do fascia boards fail during hurricanes?
Fascia boards fail because they experience the combined uplift from both surfaces - suction on top and positive pressure from below. This net uplift can exceed 70-100 PSF in Broward County's HVHZ, far exceeding what standard nailing patterns can resist. Additionally, repeated pressure cycling during extended storms fatigues connections.
What fastening is required for fascia in Broward County?
Fascia boards must be fastened to resist the calculated net uplift pressure. For typical residential fascia (1x6 or 1x8), this often requires ring-shank nails at 6" O.C. or corrosion-resistant screws. All fasteners within 3,000 feet of saltwater must be hot-dip galvanized or stainless steel. Product approvals (NOA/FBC) should specify the required fastener pattern.
How does overhang length affect uplift?
Longer overhangs generally experience higher total uplift loads because the pressure acts over a larger area. However, the pressure coefficient (GCp) is based on the effective wind area. Per ASCE 7-22, the effective wind area for overhangs uses the overhang width times a span length. Shorter overhangs may have higher unit pressures due to smaller effective areas.
Do aluminum fascia covers change the design requirements?
Aluminum fascia covers must be designed to resist the same wind pressures as the underlying fascia. However, aluminum installations have specific requirements: the cover must be secured to resist both positive and negative pressures, and the fastener schedule must account for the metal's thermal expansion. Many aluminum fascia systems have their own NOA approvals with specific installation requirements.
Is the fascia calculation different from soffit calculation?
Yes, while both are affected by overhang pressures, soffits (horizontal undersurface) primarily experience the positive bottom surface pressure, while fascia (vertical board at roof edge) must transfer the combined net uplift from both surfaces to the roof structure. Soffits also have different failure modes - they're more susceptible to blow-in from positive pressure rather than blow-out from suction.
