Exposure Category Impact

Exposure D (coastal) produces 20-40% higher wind pressures than Exposure B (suburban inland) at the same wind speed due to reduced surface roughness over open water.

Product Approval Differences

While the entire HVHZ requires approved products, coastal projects often specify Miami-Dade NOA products due to stricter testing requirements that match the higher wind loads.

Salt Air Corrosion

Coastal installations require corrosion-resistant fasteners and hardware. Stainless steel or hot-dip galvanized components are mandatory within 3,000 feet of saltwater.

Insurance Implications

Properties in coastal zones typically face higher wind insurance premiums. Proper documentation of wind mitigation features can reduce premiums by 30-50%.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I determine if my property is in the coastal or inland zone?
The coastal zone for Exposure D typically extends 1 mile from the mean high water line along the Atlantic Ocean. You can verify your exact exposure category using the ASCE 7 Hazard Tool or by consulting with Broward County Building Department. Properties in the coastal zone have unobstructed paths to the ocean with flat, open terrain.
Does the entire Broward County require impact-resistant glazing?
Yes, all of Broward County is within Florida's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ), which means all glazed openings must have impact protection or approved shutters. This applies regardless of whether you're coastal or inland. The difference is in the required design pressure ratings - coastal installations need higher DP ratings.
Can I use the same products for coastal and inland installations?
Products rated for coastal zones will work in inland areas, but not vice versa. If a product has a DP rating of +55/-85 for coastal Exposure D, it can be used in inland Exposure B/C where lower pressures apply. However, a product rated only for inland use won't meet coastal requirements. Always verify the product's tested exposure category and DP rating matches your site conditions.
What is the transition zone between coastal and inland?
ASCE 7-22 allows for a gradual transition from Exposure D to C over a distance defined by the upwind roughness length. In practice, properties 600-1500 feet from the coast may qualify for Exposure C depending on intervening buildings and vegetation. A site-specific analysis by a licensed engineer can determine if transition exposure applies.
How do flood zone requirements interact with wind zones?
Coastal properties often face overlapping wind and flood requirements. FEMA flood zones (V, VE, A, AE) add elevation and foundation requirements on top of wind design. Buildings in V zones must also account for wave action loads that combine with wind loads. Broward County coordinates these requirements through the building permit process.
