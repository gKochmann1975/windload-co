D

Dead Load (D)

Permanent loads from the weight of the structure itself, including roofing materials, framing, finishes, and fixed equipment. In Broward County, typical roof dead loads range from 10-20 PSF depending on materials.

L

Live Load (L)

Variable loads from occupancy, furniture, and temporary storage. Residential floors typically require 40 PSF, while roofs use 20 PSF minimum live load per ASCE 7-22 Table 4.3-1.

W

Wind Load (W)

Lateral and uplift forces from wind pressure. Broward County's HVHZ areas require design wind speeds of 170-180 MPH, creating pressures exceeding 50 PSF on components.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Broward County require different load combinations than other areas?
Broward County is within Florida's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ), requiring design for wind speeds of 170-180 MPH. The standard ASCE 7-22 load combinations still apply, but the magnitude of the wind load component (W) is significantly higher. This means combinations including wind loads (like 1.2D + W + L) often become the governing design case rather than gravity-only combinations.
What is the 0.6D factor used for in uplift calculations?
The 0.6D factor represents the minimum dead load that can be relied upon to resist uplift forces. During high winds, suction on the roof creates uplift that tries to lift the structure. Only 60% of the dead load is counted as resisting this uplift because some materials may be removed, deteriorated, or lighter than specified. This conservative approach ensures adequate anchorage even in worst-case scenarios.
How do I know which load combination governs my design?
You must check all applicable combinations and design for the worst case. For Broward County structures, combination 4 (1.2D + W + L) typically governs lateral systems and connections, while combination 6 (0.9D + W) often governs uplift and overturning. Use our calculator to quickly compare factored loads and identify the critical combination for your specific application.
Are there special considerations for roof-to-wall connections?
Yes, roof-to-wall connections in Broward County must resist significant uplift forces. The net uplift is calculated as the wind uplift pressure minus 0.6 times the dead load. For a typical residential roof with 15 PSF dead load and 55 PSF wind uplift, the net design uplift would be approximately 55 - (0.6 x 15) = 46 PSF. Hurricane clips or straps must be sized accordingly.
Does the Florida Building Code modify ASCE 7-22 load combinations?
The Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) adopts ASCE 7-22 with Florida-specific amendments. The basic load combination formulas remain the same, but Florida adds requirements for wind-borne debris regions, enhanced protection zones, and specific product approval requirements (FBC or Miami-Dade NOA) that affect how loads are transferred through approved products.
