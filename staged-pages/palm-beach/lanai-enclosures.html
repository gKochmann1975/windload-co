Technical Requirements

FBC 2024 specifications for lanai enclosures in Palm Beach County.

Screen Systems

  • Heavy-gauge aluminum framing required
  • Screen fabric wind ratings per section
  • Spline retention under negative pressure
  • Corner bracing at all connections
  • Continuous roof-to-foundation load path

Glass Systems

  • Impact-rated laminated glass required
  • Large missile test (9-lb 2×4 at 50 fps)
  • Miami-Dade NOA for all components
  • Structural silicone or captured glazing
  • Full pressure cycle testing

Foundation

  • Concrete slab or approved deck structure
  • Anchor bolt pattern per engineering
  • Uplift resistance verification
  • Ledger attachment to main structure
  • Waterproofing at connections

Permitting

  • PE-sealed structural calculations
  • Product approvals for all materials
  • Site plan with setback verification
  • HOA approval where applicable
  • Final inspection before use

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about lanai enclosures in Palm Beach County.

What wind loads apply to lanai enclosures in Palm Beach County?
+
Lanai enclosures in Palm Beach County HVHZ must be designed for 180+ mph wind speeds. Screen enclosures are classified as partially enclosed structures with specific internal pressure coefficients. Aluminum framing and screen fabric must resist design wind pressures typically ranging from ±25 to ±45 PSF depending on location. Glass lanais require higher ratings matching enclosed building standards.
What is the difference between a screen room and a glass lanai enclosure?
+
Screen rooms use mesh screening that allows air flow and is designed to fail safely in extreme winds, preventing catastrophic structural damage. Glass lanai enclosures use impact-rated glazing and are classified as enclosed buildings with higher structural requirements but provide full climate control. Glass enclosures cost 3-4x more but provide hurricane protection and year-round comfort.
How much does a hurricane-rated lanai enclosure cost?
+
Screen lanai enclosures range from $35-65 per square foot installed. Glass-enclosed lanais range from $150-300 per square foot. A typical 400 sq ft screen enclosure costs $14,000-26,000, while the same area with impact glass costs $60,000-120,000. Aluminum framing adds approximately 15-25% for HVHZ-rated systems versus standard construction.
Can existing lanai screens be upgraded for hurricane compliance?
+
Existing screen systems often cannot be simply upgraded because the framing must be designed as a complete system with appropriate connections to the structure. Options include replacing with heavier-gauge aluminum, adding removable hurricane fabric panels, or converting to impact glass. Full replacement is typically more cost-effective than piecemeal upgrades and ensures proper engineering.
What insurance discounts apply to lanai enclosures?
+
Insurance discounts vary by carrier and enclosure type. Glass lanai enclosures with impact-rated glazing can qualify for 5-15% premium reductions as they reduce the home's vulnerability. Screen enclosures designed for safe failure may not qualify for discounts but also don't increase premiums. Contact your insurance agent for specific credit availability before starting your project.
