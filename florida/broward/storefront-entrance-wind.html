ASCE 7-22 Section 26.12 defines enclosure classification based on the ratio of openings on dominant walls versus total enclosure openings. Vestibule design directly affects this ratio.

Storefront Entrance FAQs

Technical questions about commercial entrance systems in Broward County

What wind load rating do storefront entrances need in Broward County?
Storefront entrance systems in Broward County require design pressure ratings calculated per ASCE 7-22 for the 170 MPH design wind speed. Typical requirements range from DP +50/-60 psf for protected vestibule entries to DP +85/-110 psf for exposed corner entries. The Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) mandates product approval through the Florida Product Approval system, and all entrance assemblies must demonstrate compliance with ASTM E1886/E1996 for impact resistance in the Wind-Borne Debris Region.
How does vestibule design affect storefront entrance wind loads?
Vestibule design significantly affects wind load calculations through internal pressure coefficients. ASCE 7-22 Section 26.12 defines enclosed vs. partially enclosed classifications based on opening ratios. A properly designed vestibule with interlocking doors can reduce internal pressure coefficients from +0.55 to +0.18, potentially reducing total wind loads by 15-25%. However, if door actuators allow simultaneous opening, the building may classify as partially enclosed, increasing internal pressures substantially.
What is the difference between aluminum and steel storefront frames for wind resistance?
Aluminum storefront frames (typically 6063-T6 alloy) offer excellent corrosion resistance and lighter weight, with section modulus values supporting DP ratings up to +70/-85 psf in standard configurations. Steel frames provide 2.8x higher stiffness-to-weight ratios, enabling DP ratings of +95/-120 psf without intermediate reinforcement. For Broward's 170 MPH wind zone, aluminum frames often require wider members (2.5-inch vs 2-inch face) or horizontal mullion reinforcement to achieve equivalent ratings, adding 15-25% to material costs.
How do ADA requirements interact with hurricane-rated door hardware?
ADA Section 404.2.9 limits door opening force to 5 lbf maximum for interior doors and requires closers that close doors within 5 seconds minimum. Hurricane-rated panic hardware must simultaneously meet BHMA A156.3 Grade 1 requirements and maintain ADA force limits. This creates engineering challenges: high-wind-rated door closers typically generate 8-12 lbf closing force. Solutions include low-energy automatic operators (ANSI A156.19), balanced door systems, or electromagnetic hold-open devices that release during hurricane conditions.
What air infiltration limits apply to Broward commercial entrances?
The Florida Energy Conservation Code requires storefront entrance assemblies to limit air infiltration to 0.30 cfm per square foot at 75 Pa pressure differential (ASTM E283). For HVAC sizing and energy compliance, vestibule entrances must also meet ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Section 5.4.3.4 requirements. High-traffic entrances exceeding 3,000 operations per day typically specify air curtain systems rated at minimum 900 fpm discharge velocity to supplement weatherstripping performance.
Do storefront entrances require impact glazing in Broward County?
Yes. Broward County falls within Florida's Wind-Borne Debris Region, requiring all glazing below 60 feet to meet impact resistance per ASTM E1886/E1996. Storefront entrance glazing must pass the large missile test (9-lb 2x4 at 50 fps) and subsequent cyclic pressure testing. Options include laminated glass (typically 0.090-inch PVB interlayer minimum), polycarbonate glazing, or approved protective systems. Entrance door glass panels often require thicker laminate constructions (11/16-inch total) due to smaller lite sizes and higher localized stresses.
