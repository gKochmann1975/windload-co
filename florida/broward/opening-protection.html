Common Questions

What contractors ask about opening protection

What openings require protection in Broward County?
All glazed openings (windows, doors, skylights) must be protected in Broward County. This includes entry doors with glass, sliding glass doors, garage doors, and any opening larger than 1 inch. The HVHZ areas of Broward (east of I-95) require large missile impact testing, while non-HVHZ areas require small missile testing.
What is the difference between impact windows and shutters?
Impact windows have laminated glass built into the frame - no deployment needed before a storm. Shutters are separate products installed over standard windows that must be closed before a storm. Impact windows cost more upfront but require no action. Shutters cost less but require deployment time and storage for removable types.
Do garage doors need wind protection in Broward County?
Yes. Garage doors are the largest and most vulnerable opening in most homes. A failed garage door allows wind into the building, which can double internal pressure and blow off the roof. Broward County requires wind-rated garage doors meeting your specific design pressure, with or without impact protection depending on HVHZ status.
What is a Miami-Dade NOA and do I need one?
NOA stands for Notice of Acceptance - it is Miami-Dade County's product approval system. In Broward County HVHZ areas (east of I-95), products must have either a Miami-Dade NOA or Florida Product Approval with HVHZ designation. Non-HVHZ Broward areas can use standard Florida Building Code approved products.
Can I mix impact windows and shutters on the same building?
Yes. Many homeowners use impact windows on frequently used openings (main windows, entry doors) and shutters on less visible areas (side windows, back sliders) to balance cost and convenience. Each opening must meet its individual wind load requirement regardless of what protection method you choose.
