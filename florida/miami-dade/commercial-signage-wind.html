Ground-Mounted vs Building-Attached

Different mounting types have distinct structural and permit requirements

Ground-Mounted Signs

Monument signs, pole signs, and pylon signs require foundation engineering to resist overturning moments. The base moment equals wind force times height to center of pressure.

  • Concrete pier or spread footing foundation
  • Steel embedment depth typically 4-8 ft
  • Soil bearing capacity must be verified
  • Base plate and anchor bolt design required
  • Geotechnical report may be required for large signs
Building-Attached Signs

Wall-mounted and projecting signs transfer loads into the building structure. Connection capacity and building structural adequacy must both be verified for sign loads.

  • Through-bolts or expansion anchors in masonry
  • Embed plates or post-installed anchors in concrete
  • Connection to steel framing members preferred
  • Building must be analyzed for added load
  • Signs over 32 sq ft need PE-sealed details

Miami-Dade Sign Permit Process

Five steps from concept to approved installation

1

Calculate Wind Loads per ASCE 7-22

Determine design wind loads based on sign dimensions, height above ground, mounting type, and exposure category. Use 180 MPH ultimate wind speed (Vult) for Miami-Dade HVHZ. Calculate velocity pressure, force coefficients, and net design pressure for all sign components.

2

Design Structure and Connections

Size sign structure, support poles, and foundations to resist calculated wind loads with required safety factors. Design connection details for building-attached signs. Include wind load path from sign face through connections to foundation or building structure.

3

Prepare PE-Sealed Engineering Documents

Create structural drawings with wind load calculations sealed by a Florida-licensed Professional Engineer. For prefabricated sign systems, reference applicable Miami-Dade NOA numbers. Include material specifications, weld details, and inspection requirements.

4

Submit Complete Permit Package

Submit to Miami-Dade Building Department: zoning approval, PE-sealed structural drawings, wind load calculations, electrical plans for illuminated signs, NOA documents for prefab components, and licensed contractor information. Incomplete packages are rejected without review.

5

Schedule Required Inspections

Upon permit approval, schedule inspections: foundation/footer inspection, framing/structural inspection, electrical rough-in (for illuminated signs), and final inspection. Sign cannot be energized until final electrical inspection passes. Keep approved drawings on-site.

Commercial Signage FAQs

Common questions about sign wind loads in Miami-Dade County

What wind load do commercial signs need in Miami-Dade County?
Commercial signs in Miami-Dade County must be designed for 180 MPH ultimate wind speed per ASCE 7-22. Typical design pressures range from 45-90 psf for solid signs depending on height, exposure category, and mounting type. Ground-mounted monument signs require foundation engineering to resist overturning, while building-attached signs must demonstrate adequate structural anchorage. All signs in the HVHZ require either Miami-Dade NOA approval for prefabricated systems or PE-sealed structural engineering for custom designs.
What is the difference between solid and open sign wind loads?
Open signs (channel letters, skeleton signs with openings) experience significantly lower wind forces than solid signs because wind passes through the gaps rather than pushing against a solid surface. ASCE 7-22 Section 29.3 provides the solidity ratio calculation for determining reduced force coefficients. A sign with 40% openings might see wind loads reduced by 30-50% compared to a solid sign of the same overall dimensions. The force coefficient Cf decreases as the ratio of open area to gross area increases, making open sign designs more economical in high-wind regions like Miami-Dade.
How does sign height affect wind load calculations in Miami-Dade?
Wind velocity increases with height above ground due to reduced friction from surface obstacles. ASCE 7-22 uses velocity pressure exposure coefficients (Kz) that increase with height. In Exposure C (typical for commercial areas), Kz increases from about 0.85 at 15 feet to 1.04 at 40 feet. This means a sign mounted 30 feet high experiences approximately 15-20% higher wind pressure than the same sign at 15 feet. For tall pole signs or building-mounted signs above 60 feet, this height factor becomes a critical consideration in structural design, often requiring significantly larger support members and foundations.
Do commercial signs need permits in Miami-Dade County?
Yes. All commercial signs in Miami-Dade County require building permits with wind load calculations. Signs must comply with both the Florida Building Code (structural requirements) and local zoning regulations (size, height, setback limits). The permit package must include structural drawings, wind load analysis per ASCE 7-22 using the correct 180 MPH ultimate wind speed, foundation design for ground-mounted signs, and either a Miami-Dade NOA for prefabricated sign systems or PE-sealed engineering for custom designs. Separate electrical permits are required for all illuminated signs.
What is the force coefficient for different sign types?
ASCE 7-22 Table 29.3-1 provides force coefficients (Cf) based on sign aspect ratio, clearance ratio, and whether the sign has return walls. For solid freestanding signs, Cf ranges from 1.2 to 1.8 depending on configuration. Signs attached flush to buildings may use Cf = 1.2 for projecting signs, or alternatively apply the component and cladding provisions of Chapter 30 for wall-mounted signs. Open signs use reduced coefficients based on their solidity ratio - a sign with 30% solidity (70% open) might use Cf around 0.8-1.0, significantly reducing the design wind load compared to a solid sign.
What structural connections are required for building-attached signs?
Building-attached signs must transfer wind loads into the building structure without causing damage to either the sign or the building. Connection requirements depend on wall construction type: CMU (concrete masonry) walls typically need through-bolts or expansion anchors with pullout and shear capacity verified by calculation or testing. Tilt-up concrete walls require embedded plates during construction or post-installed mechanical anchors. Steel-framed buildings need bolted connections directly to structural members, not just cladding. The building structure itself must also be analyzed for the added load, especially for large signs that create significant overturning moments. Miami-Dade requires PE-sealed connection details for signs over 32 square feet in area.
