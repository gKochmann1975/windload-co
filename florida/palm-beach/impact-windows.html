Palm Beach Window FAQs

Common questions about impact windows in Palm Beach County

What are the wind load zones in Palm Beach County?
Palm Beach County has three main wind zones based on distance from the coast: Coastal Zone (within 1 mile of the Atlantic Ocean) with 170+ MPH design wind speed requiring DP +55 to +80, Transitional Zone (1-5 miles inland, between US-1 and I-95) with 150-165 MPH requiring DP +45 to +65, and Interior Zone (west of I-95) with 140-150 MPH requiring DP +35 to +50. Cities like Palm Beach, Jupiter, and Boca Raton's beachfront areas have the highest requirements, while Wellington and Royal Palm Beach have the lowest.
Does Palm Beach County have HVHZ requirements like Miami-Dade?
No. Palm Beach County is entirely outside the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ). All of Palm Beach County uses standard Florida Building Code requirements with small missile impact testing (2g steel balls at 130 fps) rather than Miami-Dade's large missile testing (9 lb 2x4 lumber at 50 fps). This means more window options are available and costs are typically 15-25% lower than HVHZ areas in Miami-Dade and eastern Broward. However, coastal Palm Beach areas still have stringent wind load requirements due to direct ocean exposure.
How much do impact windows cost in Palm Beach County?
Impact window costs in Palm Beach County range from $600-1,200 per window installed, averaging 15-25% less than Miami-Dade HVHZ due to lower testing requirements. Vinyl frames typically run $600-900, aluminum frames $750-1,000, and fiberglass frames $1,000-1,200 per window. A typical 2,500 sq ft home with 15-20 windows costs $12,000-24,000 for complete replacement. Coastal properties requiring higher DP ratings pay approximately 10-15% more than interior locations like Wellington or Royal Palm Beach.
What DP rating do I need for impact windows in Boca Raton?
Boca Raton impact window DP ratings depend on your distance from the coast. Beachfront properties east of A1A (Zone 1) typically need DP +55 to +75 psf. Properties between A1A and US-1 (Zone 2) need DP +45 to +60 psf. West of I-95 (Zone 3) requires DP +35 to +50 psf. Corner zones, upper floor windows, and large picture windows increase requirements above these baselines. Your wind load calculation determines the exact DP for each opening based on your specific address and building characteristics.
Are Palm Beach County permit requirements different from Miami-Dade?
Yes. Palm Beach County permits are generally faster and less complex than Miami-Dade. Key differences include: 1) No Miami-Dade NOA requirement - Florida Product Approval is sufficient, 2) Small missile impact certification is accepted throughout the county, 3) Permit review typically takes 2-5 business days compared to 5-10 in Miami-Dade, 4) Lower permit fees averaging $150-300 per project. However, you still need proper wind load calculations, approved product documentation, and a licensed contractor.
Which Palm Beach County cities have the lowest window requirements?
The western interior cities have the lowest wind load requirements: Wellington, Royal Palm Beach, Loxahatchee, and Belle Glade typically require DP +35 to +48 with 140-145 MPH design wind speeds. These areas benefit from being farthest from the coast and often have the most protection from surrounding development. The lower requirements mean more product options, faster approvals, and costs 10-20% lower than coastal areas. However, exact requirements still depend on your specific building's height, exposure, and opening sizes.
