Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about glass sizing for hurricane windows.

Why does bigger glass need to be thicker?
+
Bigger glass has more surface area for wind to push on, but the same edges to hold it. Think of a big trampoline vs a small one - the big one sags more in the middle. Larger glass lites deflect more under pressure, so they need extra thickness to stay within safe limits.
What is the maximum glass size for residential windows in Palm Beach?
+
For HVHZ residential with standard 1/4 inch laminated glass, maximum is typically around 24 x 36 inches at 60 PSF design pressure. Larger windows up to 48 x 72 inches require 3/8 inch or thicker laminated glass. Very large openings may need 1/2 inch or insulated glass units.
How do I calculate glass thickness for wind load?
+
Use ASTM E1300 charts or software. Enter the glass width, height, design pressure, and support conditions. The chart gives minimum glass thickness for a 8 per 1000 probability of breakage. For laminated glass, use the combined thickness of the glass plies only, not the interlayer.
Does aspect ratio affect glass thickness requirements?
+
Yes, square glass is stronger than long narrow glass of the same area. A 36 x 36 inch lite can be thinner than a 24 x 54 inch lite even though they have similar area. The short dimension controls deflection, so tall skinny windows need thicker glass.
