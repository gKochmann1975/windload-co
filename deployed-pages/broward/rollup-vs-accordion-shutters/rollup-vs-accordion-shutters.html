Rollup vs Accordion Shutters: Which Protects Better in Broward County?

Broward County homeowners face a pivotal choice when specifying hurricane shutters: rollup systems that retract into a compact housing box, or accordion shutters that fold along a horizontal track. Both meet FBC wind load requirements for Broward's 170-180 mph design wind speeds, but they differ dramatically in DP capacity, deployment speed, long-term maintenance, installation cost, and aesthetic impact. This comparison uses radar chart analysis across six performance criteria to help you make a data-driven decision for your property's specific needs.

Why Rollup Shutters Achieve Higher DP Ratings

The structural superiority of rollup shutters over accordion types stems from their fundamentally different load transfer mechanisms. A rollup shutter consists of interlocking extruded aluminum slats, typically 37-55mm wide, that form a continuous rigid curtain when deployed. Wind pressure distributes across the entire curtain surface and transfers through the slat interlocks directly to the vertical guide tracks on each side of the opening. There are no hinged joints, no folding seams, and no stress concentration points within the curtain itself.

Accordion shutters, by contrast, use vertically oriented blades connected by horizontal hinge pins. Each hinge creates a potential stress concentration point where the blade's resistance to bending is reduced. Under high wind loads, the accordion folds can deflect inward between hinges, creating a scalloped deformation pattern that increases air infiltration. At design pressures approaching the system's limit, these hinge-point deflections can exceed the acceptable range, causing the lock rods to disengage and the shutter to fold open mid-storm.

For Broward County's HVHZ where design wind speed reaches 180 mph, the component and cladding pressures for a typical 4x6-foot window opening reach approximately +55/-65 psf at a one-story residential height. Both shutter types meet this requirement, but rollup systems provide a safety margin of 25-40% above the minimum, while accordion systems meet code with only 5-15% margin. This difference matters during actual hurricanes, where gust factors and local pressure spikes can momentarily exceed the calculated design pressure by 15-20%.

Shutter DP Testing Requirements

  • Structural Test: 1.5x rated DP applied as uniform static pressure for 10 seconds, no permanent deformation allowed
  • Large Missile Impact: TAS 201 test with 9-lb 2x4 at 50 fps (HVHZ below 60 ft), must not penetrate curtain/blade
  • Cyclic Pressure: TAS 203 protocol with 9,000 cycles at full DP, positive and negative alternating
  • Water Infiltration: 8.0 psf pressure with simultaneous 8.8 in/hr water spray rate
  • Air Infiltration: Maximum 1.0 cfm per sq ft of shutter area at 1.57 psf test pressure
  • Operating Force: Must deploy and retract with less than 50 lbs manual force (accordion) or rated motor torque (rollup)

Broward Salt Air Maintenance Schedule

  • Rollup Tracks (Every 6 Months): Silicone spray lubricant on vertical guides, clean debris from track channels
  • Rollup Motor (Annually): Test manual override, check limit switches, verify battery backup on motorized units
  • Rollup Housing (Annually): Inspect for corrosion, clear drain holes, touch up paint on exposed surfaces
  • Accordion Tracks (Quarterly): Clear sand/debris from bottom rail, lubricate blade wheels, clean lock rod channels
  • Accordion Hinges (Quarterly): White lithium grease on all hinge pins, check for corrosion pitting on pin heads
  • Accordion Locks (Semi-Annually): Test top and bottom barrel locks for smooth engagement, replace corroded lock rods

Salt Air Corrosion: The Hidden Cost Factor

Broward County's oceanfront and Intracoastal properties expose hurricane shutters to chronic salt aerosol that accelerates corrosion on any metal component. The practical difference between rollup and accordion shutters in this environment is significant. Rollup shutters store their curtain inside a sealed housing box when not deployed, protecting the slats and their interlock mechanisms from salt exposure during the 99% of the time they are retracted. Only the guide tracks and bottom bar face constant environmental exposure.

Accordion shutters, however, remain fully exposed to the elements whether deployed or retracted. The folded blade stack sits openly beside the window, and the bottom track collects windblown sand and salt crystals that grind against the blade wheels every time the shutter is operated. In properties within 1,500 feet of the ocean in Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, and Deerfield Beach, accordion hinge pins can develop visible corrosion pitting within 2-3 years if not maintained quarterly. Once a hinge pin seizes due to corrosion, the blade cannot fold properly, and forced deployment can crack the blade at the hinge point, rendering that section of the shutter structurally compromised.

Over a 15-year lifecycle in a Broward coastal location, the total maintenance cost for accordion shutters typically runs $150-250 per year (quarterly service visits plus occasional part replacements), while rollup shutters average $100-175 per year (semi-annual service plus motor maintenance). The lower maintenance cost of rollup systems partially offsets their higher installation price, and their longer typical lifespan (20-25 years vs 15-20 years for accordion) further closes the total cost-of-ownership gap.

Which Shutter Type for Your Broward Home?

The right choice depends on your specific property, budget, and priorities. Here is a decision framework based on common Broward County homeowner scenarios.

Choose Rollup Shutters When:

Your property is within 2,000 feet of the ocean or Intracoastal, where salt air demands the sealed protection of a housing box. You travel frequently and want remote deployment capability through smart home integration. Your HOA or condo association requires concealed shutters that are invisible when retracted. You want maximum DP ratings for peace of mind beyond the minimum code requirement. You plan to stay in the home long-term, where the higher upfront cost amortizes over 20+ years of reliable service. You have standard-width openings (under 12 feet) where rollup systems excel without needing intermediate guides.

Choose Accordion Shutters When:

Budget is the primary constraint and you need to protect many openings at the lowest cost per square foot. You have wide lanai openings, pool enclosure entries, or sliding glass doors spanning 15-20 feet where accordion's continuous track capability eliminates the need for intermediate supports. You want simple, tool-free deployment that any family member can handle without motorized systems. Your property is in western Broward (Coral Springs, Parkland, Weston) where reduced salt exposure lessens the corrosion maintenance differential. You are protecting a rental property where the lower capital cost improves your investment return, and tenants can easily operate the manual system.

Rollup vs Accordion FAQs

Answers to the most common comparison questions from Broward County homeowners choosing between rollup and accordion hurricane shutters.

Which has a higher DP rating in Broward County: rollup or accordion shutters?

+
Rollup shutters generally achieve higher DP ratings than accordion shutters in Broward County installations. Quality rollup systems from major manufacturers can reach DP ratings of +75/-90 psf or higher, while accordion shutters typically max out around DP +55/-65 psf. The difference comes from the rollup design: extruded aluminum slats interlock into a continuous rigid curtain with no hinged joints, distributing wind load evenly across the full span. Accordion shutters transfer load through folding blade hinges that create stress concentration points. For Broward's HVHZ zones requiring 180 mph design wind speed, both types meet code for most residential openings, but rollup shutters provide a significantly larger safety margin above the minimum required DP.

How fast can each shutter type be deployed before a hurricane in Broward?

+
Rollup shutters deploy in 30-90 seconds per opening using a motorized control or manual crank, with no external tools or hardware required. A typical Broward home with 12 openings can be fully shuttered in under 20 minutes by one person. Accordion shutters require 2-5 minutes per opening to unfold, align the blades in the tracks, and engage the locking bolts at top and bottom. The same 12-opening home takes 30-60 minutes with accordion shutters. The deployment speed advantage of rollup systems becomes critical during rapid hurricane intensification events, which have become more frequent in South Florida, where the window between warning and landfall can shrink to less than 24 hours.

What is the cost difference between rollup and accordion shutters per opening?

+
Accordion shutters cost approximately $25-45 per square foot of opening area installed in Broward County, making them the more budget-friendly option. A standard 6x4-foot window costs $600-1,080 for accordion protection. Rollup shutters cost $45-85 per square foot installed, so the same window runs $1,080-2,040. For a typical Broward home with 1,200 square feet of total opening area, the difference can be $24,000-48,000 between the two systems. However, the total cost of ownership calculation should factor in rollup's higher insurance discount, longer lifespan, and lower annual maintenance costs in coastal environments. Over a 20-year ownership period, the net cost difference narrows to approximately $15,000-25,000 for a typical home.

Do rollup and accordion shutters have different maintenance requirements?

+
Yes, and the difference is magnified by Broward County's salt air environment. Rollup shutters need semi-annual track lubrication, annual motor inspection for motorized units, and periodic bottom bar weatherseal replacement every 5-7 years. The housing box protects the curtain from environmental exposure. Accordion shutters require quarterly track and hinge lubrication because the blades ride in exposed bottom tracks that collect sand, salt, and debris. Hinge pins between blades can corrode and seize in salt air, making deployment difficult or impossible when needed most. For properties within 1,500 feet of the ocean, the maintenance differential is most pronounced, with accordion shutters needing roughly twice the annual service attention of rollup systems.

Which shutter type works better for sliding glass doors in Broward County?

+
For sliding glass doors and wide openings, accordion shutters hold the advantage. They can span up to 20+ feet in a single continuous track run, making them ideal for lanai openings, pool deck sliders, and the wide glass walls common in Broward waterfront homes. Rollup shutters work well for standard sliders up to 12-16 feet but require a housing box above the opening that adds 10-14 inches of vertical bulk, which can conflict with soffits and architectural details. For openings wider than 12 feet, rollup systems may need intermediate guide rails that bisect the view. Accordion shutters fold compactly to one or both sides with no vertical bulk above the opening, preserving the architectural intent of wide-span glass designs.

Are there HOA restrictions on rollup vs accordion shutters in Broward?

+
Many Broward County HOAs and condo associations have specific requirements that favor one type. Oceanfront condos in Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, and Pompano Beach frequently mandate rollup shutters because the housing box can be integrated into the wall or soffit, making the shutter invisible when retracted. Accordion shutters remain visible as a stacked blade assembly beside each opening, which some HOAs prohibit on street-facing or ocean-facing elevations for aesthetic reasons. However, Florida Statute 718.113 prevents condo associations from prohibiting hurricane protection entirely, and the statute was strengthened after Hurricane Andrew to prevent HOA obstruction of shutter installation. If your HOA has restrictions, rollup shutters generally satisfy even the strictest aesthetic guidelines.
